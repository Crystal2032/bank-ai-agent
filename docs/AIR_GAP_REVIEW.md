## Official Security Baseline

| Tier | Official policy | Project treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Green | Pure balance/history/bill/return queries run automatically after intent identification | Authenticated scoped reads, without blanket reconfirmation |
| Yellow | Explicit detail review and confirmation; daily cumulative transfers <= CNY 1,000, subscription query/cancellation, virtual-card application | Retain yellow subscription lookup even though it is a read. Owner additionally requires fresh Passkey/password verification for every payment |
| Red | Strong verification; daily cumulative transfers > CNY 1,000, password changes, lost-card reporting/restoration, investment purchase; examples include face, SMS, or U-key multi-factor authentication | Password alone is insufficient. The designed user-verifying Passkey route is our proposed implementation; organizer acceptance as the required strong verification is unresolved. Password plus action-bound SMS is the proposed fallback |

This threshold applies to daily cumulative transfer amount, not just one transfer. Atomic reservations and execution-time risk checks already address splitting/concurrency in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md). Passkey recovery is owner-required. The October 4 policy in AUTHENTICATOR.md supersedes earlier hold proposals: adaptive backup recovery and no routine funds waiting period.

The official security requirements are high priority. Neither a warning in the prompt nor a successful Passkey ceremony demonstrates hallucination protection, injection defense, or sandbox isolation.

## Recommended Pi-Based Architecture

Keep one TypeScript backend with explicit modules. The following responsibilities adapt AIR's public patterns to the team's scope; they are our design proposals, not a reconstruction of AIR internals.

1. **Pi Agent / Pi AI:** reuse the generic loop, streaming, tool contracts, and model interface. No bank credentials, authentication-factor tools, arbitrary shell, or extension auto-loading.
2. **Domain modules:** transfers, bills, subscriptions own typed contracts and evidence semantics. One orchestrator selects these tool groups. AIR's distributed sub-agent ownership motivates modularity; it does not require us to add multiple autonomous agents.
3. **Task coordinator:** persist and validate the business DAG, pending approvals, leases, and dependencies. Agent turns are short-lived; banking state survives model failures and process restarts.
4. **Shared execution gate:** derive identity; validate entitlement, risk, factors, account restrictions, balance, reservation, and idempotency immediately before dispatch. Direct client actions use the same gate.
5. **Security service:** implements the owner's transaction verification and Passkey recovery outside the model. Do not equate WebAuthn user verification with bank face-recognition/KYC or assume biometric availability on every device.
6. **Evidence projector and client events:** assemble safe structured progress, account facts, and receipts. The assistant may explain evidence but cannot redefine it. Do not stream unvalidated financial-success claims into the trusted result view.
7. **Model policy and quality controls:** wrap Pi AI with a small configuration/policy module, telemetry and regression evaluation. A standalone gateway service, vector database, custom foundation model, and microservice estate are not required by this scope.

An example completed flow is: analyze spending -> identify recurring-charge candidates -> let the customer choose -> confirm yellow subscription lookup/cancellation -> resolve a transfer recipient -> review and verify the exact transfer -> dispatch once -> display canonical receipts. Dependency failures stop only dependent steps; cancellation reports which steps already completed. Returning to a prior UI step does not reverse a settled transfer. Any compensation is a distinct authorized operation supported by the provider, not an assumed ability.

## Evaluation and Submission Evidence

Build an evaluation manifest with fixture version, initial state, input turns, expected task graph/clarification, expected risk/approval, final ledger/subscription state, permitted evidence claims, and expected failure response. Record model/prompt/tool/policy configuration and execution traces for reproducibility.

- Deterministic checks: ownership, approval replay/changes, CNY 1,000/1,000.01 boundaries, concurrent reservations, recovery restrictions, idempotency, DAG dependencies, restart/takeover, and unknown-result reconciliation.
- Model-task checks: paraphrases, omitted references, conflicting follow-ups, ambiguous recipients, invalid assumptions, unsupported requests, and cross-domain planning. Score task success against backend state, not the model's own assertion of success.
- Adversarial checks: direct/indirect injection, spoofed provider messages, fabricated receipt references, cross-customer retrieval, and forbidden tool/output channels. An LLM judge may assist explanation scoring but cannot decide money or authorization correctness.
- Reliability checks: model/provider timeout, malformed result, client disconnect, executor crash, post-commit response loss, and audit persistence failure. Pre-dispatch audit/persistence failure blocks new writes; post-commit failures preserve and reconcile the operation instead of declaring it absent.

Tie the submission's architecture, test evidence, demo, and security self-assessment to the same requirement IDs. Keep known risks and unresolved acceptance explicit. Avoid reproducing AIR's legal language, external-provider privacy claims, or support statistics as our own guarantees.
